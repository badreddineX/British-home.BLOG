Moving into your first rented flat is one of those milestones that feels exciting until you realise nobody teaches you how to actually do it. The keys are in your hand, the flat is empty, and there is a surprisingly long list of practical tasks between "I have a flat" and "I live here comfortably."

This checklist covers everything a first-time renter in the UK needs to handle, organised by when to do it: before move-in day, on the day itself, and during the first week.

## Before Move-In Day

### Confirm Your Deposit Protection

Your landlord or letting agent is legally required to place your deposit in one of three government-backed tenancy deposit schemes within 30 days of receiving it:

- **Tenancy Deposit Scheme (TDS)**
- **Deposit Protection Service (DPS)**
- **mydeposits**

They must also provide you with the scheme's prescribed information, including which scheme holds your deposit and how to dispute deductions at the end of the tenancy. If your landlord fails to protect your deposit, you can claim up to three times the deposit amount through the courts.

**Action:** Ask your landlord or agent which scheme your deposit is registered with and request the certificate.

### Set Up Energy

Your flat will already have a gas and electricity supplier. You are not obligated to stay with them.

**Action:**
1. Ask your landlord or agent who the current energy supplier is, or check the meters for a supplier sticker.
2. Contact the supplier to register in your name and provide your move-in date.
3. Compare tariffs on a comparison site if you want to switch — you are legally free to choose any supplier.

### Book Broadband

Broadband installation can take one to two weeks, so start this before you move.

**Action:**
1. Check which providers serve your postcode and what speeds are available.
2. Compare deals — most providers offer 12 to 24 month contracts.
3. Book installation and confirm whether an engineer visit is needed.

### Arrange Contents Insurance

Your landlord's buildings insurance covers the structure of the property. It does not cover your belongings. Contents insurance protects your possessions against theft, fire, flood, accidental damage, and (with some policies) damage to the landlord's property caused by you.

**Cost:** Basic cover starts at £5 to £8 per month. Comprehensive cover with accidental damage runs £10 to £20 per month.

**Action:** Compare quotes online. Many providers offer specific "tenant" or "renter" policies.

### Water Rates

In most areas, you will need to register with your local water company. In some cases, the landlord includes water rates in the rent. Check your tenancy agreement.

**Action:** Contact the water company serving your address to register. If you have a water meter, take a reading on move-in day.

### Council Tax

You are responsible for council tax unless your tenancy agreement states otherwise (rare, but sometimes included in HMO arrangements).

**Action:**
1. Contact your local council to register at your new address.
2. Check if you qualify for any reductions: single person discount (25% off), full-time student exemption (100% off), or low-income Council Tax Reduction.

### TV Licence

If you watch live TV on any channel, use BBC iPlayer, or record programmes as they are broadcast, you need a TV licence (£169.50 per year as of 2026).

**Action:** Set up or transfer your TV licence at tvlicensing.co.uk. If you only stream non-BBC services (Netflix, Disney+, YouTube) and never watch live TV, you do not need one — but you must declare this.

## Day-One Essentials: What to Buy Before You Arrive

These items should be in the flat on move-in day. Total cost: approximately £50 to £120.

### Bathroom

- [ ] Toilet roll (multipack)
- [ ] Hand soap
- [ ] Bath towels (at least 2) and hand towels (at least 2)
- [ ] Shower curtain (if needed — check with agent)
- [ ] Basic cleaning spray and cloth

### Kitchen

- [ ] Kettle (£10 to £25 — your most important day-one purchase)
- [ ] Tea, coffee, milk, sugar
- [ ] Washing-up liquid and a sponge
- [ ] Bin bags
- [ ] One mug, one plate, one bowl, cutlery for one
- [ ] A saucepan and a frying pan (if not supplied)

### Bedroom

- [ ] Bedding: fitted sheet, duvet, duvet cover, pillows, pillowcases
- [ ] If no bed is provided: an air mattress for the first night (£15 to £30) while your real mattress is delivered

### General

- [ ] Lightbulbs (check what fittings the flat uses — bayonet or screw, and wattage)
- [ ] A basic toolkit: screwdriver, hammer, tape measure, picture hooks
- [ ] Extension lead with surge protector
- [ ] Phone charger and cable
- [ ] First aid kit

## On Move-In Day

### Take Meter Readings

This is critically important. Without move-in readings, you may be charged for the previous tenant's energy use.

**Action:**
1. Locate the gas meter and electricity meter. They may be inside the flat, in a communal cupboard, or in an external meter box.
2. Photograph each meter showing the reading and date.
3. Report the readings to your energy supplier immediately.

### Complete the Inventory and Condition Report

Most letting agents provide an inventory or check-in report listing the condition of the property and its contents. This is the document your deposit will be assessed against when you leave.

**Action:**
1. Go through the inventory carefully, room by room.
2. Note any discrepancies: existing damage, stains, marks, broken items, or anything missing from the list.
3. Add your notes to the report and return it to the agent within the specified timeframe (usually 7 days).

### Photograph Everything

Even if you complete the inventory report, take your own dated photographs.

**Action:**
1. Photograph every room from multiple angles.
2. Photograph any existing damage: marks on walls, stains on carpets, scratches on worktops, chips on tiles, limescale in the bathroom.
3. Photograph the condition of appliances, the oven interior, and window frames.
4. Store these photographs in a dedicated folder and back them up.

### Test Everything

**Action:**
- [ ] Hot water runs from all taps
- [ ] Heating works (turn it on briefly even in summer)
- [ ] All hob rings and the oven work
- [ ] Extractor fans work in the kitchen and bathroom
- [ ] All windows open and close properly
- [ ] All door locks work (front door, back door, windows)
- [ ] Smoke alarms are present and working (press the test button)
- [ ] Carbon monoxide alarm is present if gas appliances exist (legal requirement since 2022)
- [ ] Toilet flushes properly
- [ ] Shower has adequate pressure
- [ ] All plug sockets work (use your phone charger to test)
- [ ] Light switches work in every room

Report any issues to your landlord or agent in writing on day one.

### Locate Key Information

**Action:**
- [ ] Find the fuse box (consumer unit) and know how to reset a tripped switch
- [ ] Find the water stopcock (usually under the kitchen sink) and check it turns
- [ ] Find the gas shut-off valve (usually next to the gas meter)
- [ ] Note the location of fire exits and assembly points (for flats)
- [ ] Get emergency contact numbers: landlord, agent, and building management (if applicable)

## First Week

### Register with a GP

NHS GP registration can take time. Do this early.

**Action:** Find your nearest GP surgery accepting new patients at nhs.uk. Bring photo ID and proof of address (your tenancy agreement works).

### Set Up Post Redirection

If you are moving from a previous address, redirect your post through Royal Mail (from £35.99 for 3 months).

**Action:** Set up at royalmail.com/redirection. It takes 5 to 10 working days to start.

### Register to Vote

You must register to vote at your new address. This also affects your credit score.

**Action:** Register online at gov.uk/register-to-vote. It takes about 5 minutes.

### Update Your Address

**Action:**
- [ ] Bank and credit cards
- [ ] DVLA (driving licence and vehicle registration)
- [ ] Employer or student finance
- [ ] Subscriptions and deliveries
- [ ] Dentist
- [ ] Phone provider
- [ ] Insurance policies

### Meet Your Neighbours

This is optional but recommended, especially in a flat. A brief introduction goes a long way if you ever need to coordinate about noise, deliveries, or shared spaces.

### Understand Your Tenancy Agreement

Read your tenancy agreement fully. Pay particular attention to:

- **Notice period** — how much notice you must give to leave
- **Break clause** — whether you can leave before the fixed term ends, and when
- **Restrictions** — pets, guests staying overnight, subletting, hanging items on walls
- **Maintenance responsibilities** — what the landlord covers versus what you are expected to handle (usually minor items like replacing lightbulbs and batteries)
- **Rent review clauses** — whether and when rent can increase during the tenancy

## Ongoing: Monthly Habits

- [ ] Check bank statement for correct rent amount leaving your account
- [ ] Test smoke and CO alarms monthly (press the test button)
- [ ] Check for damp or mould in corners, window frames, and behind furniture — report early
- [ ] Keep a folder (physical or digital) of all tenancy correspondence

---

Moving into your first flat can feel overwhelming, but most of the admin takes a few hours spread across the first week. The two most important things are: take photographs and meter readings before you unpack, and put everything in writing. If you do those two things, you have protected yourself for the entire tenancy.
