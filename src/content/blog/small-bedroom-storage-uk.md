The bedroom in a small UK flat has to do double duty: sleep and storage. Most small bedrooms do the sleep part reasonably well and the storage part badly — the result is a room that always feels cluttered and slightly too small. After renting small bedrooms in London and Manchester, the under-bed and wardrobe changes in this list are the ones I'd make first — they cost under £25 combined and the difference is immediate. Here is the complete approach to bedroom storage, in the order that makes the most difference.

**TL;DR:** Start with under-bed storage (IKEA SKUBB bags, ~£6) and slim velvet hangers (~£15, Amazon UK) — together these two changes can transform a small bedroom for under £25. Add a tension rod for double hanging (£12) and an over-door wardrobe organiser (£18-£25). The IKEA NORDLI storage bed (from £350) replaces the chest of drawers entirely and is the most impactful single furniture purchase for a small bedroom.

---

## 1. What's the Best Way to Use Under-Bed Storage First?

The area under the bed is the largest storage zone in most bedrooms, and it is almost always underused.

IKEA SKUBB under-bed bags cost just £6 for two and, alongside £18 bed risers to add clearance, turn the largest underused storage zone in most bedrooms into functional space in minutes.

**Options:**
- **IKEA SKUBB under-bed bags** (~£6 for 2) — flat, zippable, dust-resistant. Ideal for off-season clothes and extra bedding. Available at IKEA UK
- **Flat lidded boxes** — available at Amazon UK, Wilko, or IKEA. Choose clear boxes so you can see contents without opening
- **Bed risers** — if your bed frame sits too low (~£18 at Amazon UK), risers add 15cm of clearance

**What to store under the bed:** off-season clothing, extra bed linens, bulky items used rarely (winter duvet in summer, summer bedding in winter), shoes.

## 2. Why Do Slim Velvet Hangers Make Such a Big Difference in the Wardrobe?

Switching from plastic to slim velvet hangers is the highest-value wardrobe upgrade available. Plastic hangers are 2-2.5cm thick. Velvet hangers are 0.5cm thick. Replacing 30 hangers frees 45-60cm of rail space — room for 15-20 additional garments.

**Cost:** Amazon UK, 50-pack ~£15.

## 3. Tension Rod for Double Hanging

A second tension rod installed below the main wardrobe rail creates a full second tier for short items — shirts, folded blazers, folded trousers, jackets. Five minutes to install, no tools required.

**Cost:** Amazon UK, tension rod ~£12.

## 4. How Does an Over-Door Wardrobe Organiser Help?

The back of the bedroom wardrobe door is typically empty. A 24-pocket over-door organiser holds shoes, accessories, belts, scarves, and socks cleanly.

A 24-pocket over-door organiser costs around £18-£25 and converts an entirely empty door into storage for shoes, belts, scarves, and socks.

**Cost:** Amazon UK, ~£18-£25.

## 5. Is a Storage Bed Frame Worth Buying?

IKEA NORDLI bed frame with built-in drawers (~£350-£600, single to double) has large drawers on each side — enough to replace a full chest of drawers in most small bedrooms. Removing the chest of drawers frees significant floor space and makes the room feel larger.

This is the most impactful single furniture purchase for a small bedroom.

## 6. Can Floating Shelves Work as a Nightstand?

A bedside table takes 40-50cm x 40cm of floor space. A floating shelf (IKEA LACK, ~£9) mounted at nightstand height with Command strips provides the same function with zero floor footprint.

Two floating shelves — one on each side of the bed — cost ~£18-£20 and free a meaningful amount of floor space in a small room.

## 7. Shelf Dividers for Folded Clothes

Stacks of folded jumpers, jeans, and t-shirts on wardrobe shelves always topple. Shelf dividers clip onto any shelf and keep each stack in its lane.

**Cost:** Amazon UK, 6-pack ~£15-£22.

## 8. A Wall-Mounted Mirror With Storage

A mirror that doubles as a storage cabinet is a classic small-flat solution. Wall-mounted mirrored jewellery cabinets open to reveal storage for accessories.

**Cost:** Amazon UK or Wayfair UK, ~£65-£120.

## 9. What Can You Store With Over-the-Door Hooks on the Bedroom Door?

The bedroom door itself is storage space. An over-door hook rack or row of Command hooks holds a dressing gown, tomorrow's outfit, and handbags.

**Cost:** Amazon UK over-door hook rack, ~£12-£20. Command large hooks ~£10 for a 4-pack.

## 10. Vacuum Storage Bags for Seasonal Clothes

Off-season clothes are the primary driver of wardrobe overflow in a UK flat. Heavy winter coats, wellies, and jumpers compress to a quarter of their normal size in vacuum storage bags.

**Cost:** Amazon UK, 6-bag set ~£16-£20.

## 11. What's the Best Wardrobe Option for Flats Without Built-In Storage?

Some older conversions and studio flats have inadequate or no bedroom wardrobe space. An IKEA PAX wardrobe system solves this completely: it's freestanding, customisable, and provides more organised storage than most built-in wardrobes.

A 100cm wide x 201cm tall PAX with interior organisers stores a full wardrobe for one person.

**Cost:** Frame from ~£95, full configuration with doors and organisers ~£300-£550.

## 12. A Tall Chest of Drawers Instead of a Wide One

If you keep a chest of drawers, choose a tall one (5-6 drawers high) rather than a wide one (3-4 drawers across). Tall chests use vertical space, leaving more floor footprint for movement. Wide, low chests consume floor space without adding much capacity.

## 13. How Do Wicker Baskets Keep Open Shelves Accessible and Tidy?

If you have open shelves in the bedroom (BILLY bookcase, floating shelves, wardrobe shelves without doors), wicker baskets hide contents while keeping them accessible. One basket per category: scarves, gym kit, bags, and so on.

**Cost:** Wicker baskets from HomeSense, IKEA UK, or Amazon UK, ~£8-£20 each.

Bedroom storage is a sequence problem: wardrobe first (hangers, tension rod, over-door organiser), then under-bed space, then wall storage. Get the basics working before investing in furniture changes.

## Bedroom Storage for Two People in a Small UK Flat

Sharing a small bedroom with a partner doubles the storage challenge. These solutions are specifically designed for two people in a single small bedroom:

**Divide the wardrobe in half — literally:** Use a tension rod or a simple hanging divider to split the wardrobe cleanly. Each person has one side. This prevents the wardrobe drift that causes chaos in shared spaces.

**Two over-door organisers:** One on each side of the wardrobe door, or one on the wardrobe door and one on the bedroom door. Each person gets their own organiser for shoes, accessories, and daily items — no rummaging through each other's things.

**A chest of drawers each, stacked where possible:** Two slim 3-drawer chests stacked vertically take the same floor footprint as one wide chest while providing double the drawer capacity. IKEA NORDLI drawers are stackable and modular.

**Separate under-bed zones:** Use a bed with built-in drawers (IKEA NORDLI) where each person owns one side of the drawers. If using flat boxes, use different coloured boxes per person or label them clearly.

**A shared clothing edit once per season:** The primary source of wardrobe overflow for two people is clothing that no longer fits or gets worn. A seasonal edit — 30 minutes per person per season — prevents the wardrobe from slowly filling with unused items. British Heart Foundation and most charity shops accept donations year-round.

## UK Winter Bedroom Storage

British winters require seasonal storage management. Here's the specific winter approach for UK flat bedrooms:

**Heavy coat storage:** Winter coats don't belong in the bedroom wardrobe year-round. From May through September, store winter coats in vacuum storage bags (~£16-£20, Amazon UK) compressed to quarter size, then placed under the bed.

**Wellies and boots storage:** Wet winter boots need a ventilated space to dry — not the bedroom wardrobe. A boot tray at the entry with a slim shoe rack handles in-season boots. Off-season footwear goes into clear shoe boxes under the bed.

**Winter bedding rotation:** Summer duvet and lightweight blankets go into SKUBB under-bed bags from October through April. Heavy winter duvet and flannel sheets stay on the bed. This single swap frees a significant amount of shelf space in the airing cupboard.

**Heating season tip:** Keep the wardrobe door slightly ajar if possible. UK flats with central heating can get warm, and a slightly open wardrobe door allows air circulation that prevents musty smells in stored clothing over a long winter.
