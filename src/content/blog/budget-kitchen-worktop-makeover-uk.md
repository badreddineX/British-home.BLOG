A worn kitchen worktop has three fix options at three different price points: wrap film from £20-£30 for a cosmetic cover-up, specialist paint from £30-£45 for a different finish, or a full laminate replacement from £90-£180 DIY if the damage goes deeper than the surface.

Which one makes sense depends on what's actually wrong with the worktop — a faded colour and light scuffs are a different problem to swelling at the sink cut-out, and only one of the three options below fixes the second.

---

## Check the Damage Before Choosing a Fix

Wrap film and paint are cosmetic fixes — they change how the worktop looks, not its underlying condition. Before picking either, check for:

- **Swelling or softness** around the sink or hob, usually from water getting into the chipboard core under the laminate. This needs replacing, not covering.
- **Deep scratches or gouges** that catch a fingernail. Wrap film will show the texture through it; paint may fill shallow ones but not deep gouges.
- **Burn marks** near the hob. Small ones can sometimes be sanded and filled; larger ones usually need the damaged section replaced.

If the worktop is structurally sound and the problem is purely cosmetic — faded colour, surface scuffs, dated pattern — wrap film or paint will do the job.

## Option 1: Worktop Wrap Film

Wrap film (£20-£30 a roll, enough for a standard 3-metre run) is a thick, adhesive-backed vinyl that goes directly over an existing laminate worktop. It's the cheapest option here and the fastest — a full worktop takes an afternoon with just a craft knife, a squeegee, and a heat gun or hairdryer for the edges.

**What it's good for:** a flat, undamaged laminate worktop that's just dated or faded. Wood-effect and stone-effect wrap films both look convincing from a normal viewing distance.

**What it won't do:** survive direct heat from pans (always use a trivet), or hide swelling, deep gouges, or texture underneath — the film follows the surface it's applied to rather than smoothing it out.

## Option 2: Specialist Worktop Paint

Two-part specialist worktop paint (£30-£45 for enough to cover a standard kitchen) gives a stone or granite-effect finish over laminate and some other surfaces. Unlike wrap film, it needs a full cure time, often several days, before the worktop can be used normally — check the product's instructions before committing to a timeline.

**What it's good for:** a worktop you want to change colour entirely, particularly toward a stone-look finish that wrap film in the same style can sometimes look less convincing in.

**What it won't do:** resist chipping at the front edge and corners as well as wrap film or a real surface, since paint is more brittle under repeated knocks than vinyl.

## Option 3: Full Laminate Worktop Replacement

A DIY laminate worktop replacement costs roughly £90-£180 in materials for a standard 3-metre run, cut to size by the supplier (B&Q, Wickes, and Worktop Express all offer made-to-measure cutting) and fitted yourself with basic tools. This is the only option that fully resets the surface rather than covering what's already there.

**What it's good for:** any worktop with structural damage — swelling, deep burns, cracked edges — or where you want a genuinely different material, not just a different film or paint finish.

**What it won't do:** come close to wrap film or paint on cost. A professionally fitted laminate worktop runs £300-£600 including labour, and stone or quartz replacements start considerably higher, from £800 upward for the same run.

## Which Option Fits Your Situation?

**Renting, worktop is sound but dated:** wrap film. Fully reversible, cheapest option, and nothing is damaged if you need to remove it before moving out.

**Own the place, want a stone-look finish without a full replacement:** worktop paint, provided you can work around the cure time.

**Any swelling, deep damage, or you want a genuinely different material:** skip straight to replacement — wrap and paint will only disappoint on damaged surfaces.
