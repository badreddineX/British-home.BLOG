The living room colour that works depends on which way your windows face, more than anything else: north-facing rooms need warm shades like Dulux Goose Down or Dulux Heritage Rich Mahogany; south and west-facing rooms can carry cooler tones like Little Greene Dock Blue or a true grey without looking cold.

Most living room colour advice ignores this and is written for a converted barn with floor-to-ceiling south-facing glass. Yours is probably a north-facing Victorian terrace, a new-build with one window, or a rented flat where painting isn't on the table at all. Below are 11 living room colour schemes grouped by room aspect, with named Dulux and Farrow & Ball paints rather than vague colour categories. If you're renting, skip to the no-paint version near the end.

---

## Check Your Light Before You Check a Paint Chart

The single biggest mistake in picking a living room colour scheme is choosing it under a paint chart's lighting instead of your own. **North-facing lounges** get flat, cool, consistent light all day with no direct sun — cool greys and blues that look elegant in a show home can turn genuinely grey and lifeless here. **South and west-facing rooms** get warmer, more direct light, so they can carry cooler tones — soft blues, true greys, even a cool white — without looking cold. **East-facing rooms** get a cooler, brighter light in the morning that warms slightly through the day — treat these as a middle ground.

If you're not sure which way your windows face, check a compass app or just notice what time of day the room gets direct sun. Two minutes now saves a repaint in six weeks.

## 11 Living Room Colour Schemes by Room Type

### 1. Warm Neutral — North-Facing Terrace

Dulux Goose Down on the walls (70%), rust or burnt orange curtains and a jute rug (20%), brass and mustard accents (10%). Warms up flat northern light without tipping into "orange room."

### 2. Soft Greige — Any Aspect, Small Room

Farrow & Ball Elephant's Breath, a warm greige that flatters both north and south-facing rooms. Works especially well as the 70% base in a small lounge where you don't want the walls competing with furniture.

### 3. Cool Grey — South-Facing New-Build

A cool-leaning pale grey or warm white wall (70%), navy or bottle green sofa or armchair (20%), warm wood and a single brass accent (10%). The room's own light carries the cooler base without it reading as cold.

### 4. Deep Green Feature Wall

Farrow & Ball Studio Green behind the sofa or around a fireplace, kept to one wall in a room with decent daylight elsewhere. A muted, slightly khaki green that reads as sophisticated rather than dark, especially paired with warm wood furniture.

### 5. Rich Mahogany — North-Facing Rooms With a Fireplace

Dulux Heritage Rich Mahogany, a warm, deep red-brown, is strong behind a sofa or chimney breast and particularly effective in rooms that already get a cosy, low-light feel in the evening.

### 6. Chalky Navy — Low-Light Lounges

Little Greene Dock Blue, a muted, chalky navy that works even in rooms with limited natural light, since it's dark enough not to show the room's lack of brightness as a flaw.

### 7. Warm Taupe — East-Facing Rooms

A warm taupe base balances the shift from cool morning light to warmer afternoon light in an east-facing lounge. Crown Fossil is a lighter, warmer alternative if the room is also on the small side.

### 8. Sage and Rust — South-Facing, Good Light

A soft sage wall colour with rust or terracotta soft furnishings. Works particularly well in rooms with enough daylight to carry a mid-tone green without it feeling heavy.

### 9. Monochrome Warm Neutral — Very Small Lounges

Keep walls, large furniture and even the rug in close shades of one warm neutral (closer to 80% of the room), with one secondary colour through curtains and one accent colour used sparingly. Fewer competing colours reads as calmer in under 12 square metres.

### 10. Dark and Moody — South or West-Facing Only

A near-black or deep charcoal wall, reserved for rooms with strong natural light during the day, since the drama depends on contrast rather than concealing a lack of light. Pair with warm brass lighting for evening.

### 11. Pastel and Natural Wood — Scandi-Leaning

A pale, slightly warm pastel (dusty pink, soft sage, or pale blue) paired with light natural wood furniture and a jute or sisal rug. Works across most aspects because the pastel is light enough not to need strong daylight to read well.

| Scheme | Paint pick | Best aspect |
|---|---|---|
| Warm neutral | Dulux Goose Down | North-facing |
| Soft greige | Farrow & Ball Elephant's Breath | Any, small rooms |
| Cool grey | A pale cool grey or warm white | South-facing |
| Deep green | Farrow & Ball Studio Green | Good daylight, feature wall |
| Rich mahogany | Dulux Heritage Rich Mahogany | North-facing, with a fireplace |
| Chalky navy | Little Greene Dock Blue | Low natural light |
| Warm taupe | Crown Fossil | East-facing |

**Prices:** a 2.5L tin of Dulux Heritage runs £45-£50 at B&Q; Farrow & Ball's equivalent size is closer to £62-£70. A single feature wall in a standard UK lounge (roughly 12-15 square metres of wall) needs one tin.

**Buy a sample pot first.** Dulux and Farrow & Ball sample pots cost £5-£8. Paint a 30cm square in at least two spots — one near the window, one on the darkest wall — and check it morning, afternoon, and with the lamps on in the evening.

## What If You Can't Paint at All?

If you're renting and painting isn't an option, you can get most of the effect of any of the schemes above through soft furnishings alone, and none of it needs landlord permission. See [renter-friendly living room ideas UK](/blog/renter-friendly-living-room-ideas-uk/) for the full no-drilling approach.

**A full colour layer without touching a wall, roughly £120-£180:**

| Item | Typical cost | Role |
|---|---|---|
| A patterned or coloured rug | £45-£80 (Dunelm, IKEA) | Secondary colour, anchors the room |
| 3-4 cushions in a secondary/accent shade | £8-£15 each | Secondary + accent |
| A throw over the sofa arm | £15-£25 | Secondary |
| Curtains in your accent or secondary colour | £25-£50 a pair (Dunelm) | Secondary colour, large surface |

This gets you most of the visual impact of a painted feature wall, because a rug and curtains cover a comparable surface area to a wall, without a single tin of paint. If your landlord does allow a feature wall, [peel-and-stick wallpaper](/blog/renter-friendly-living-room-ideas-uk/) is the middle ground: full colour or pattern for £15-£40 a roll, removable at the end of the tenancy.

## Small Living Room? Keep the Base Light

In a small UK living room, the 70% base colour matters more than the accents, because there's less wall for the accent colours to sit against. Our guide to [small living room ideas UK](/blog/small-living-room-ideas-uk/) covers the layout tricks — curtain height, mirror placement, rug sizing — that work alongside any of these colour schemes to make a small room read as bigger, not just more colourful.

---

Pick your scheme from your room's light and aspect first, not from a photo of someone else's lounge. Everything else — the rug, the curtains, the cushions — is easier to get right once that's settled, and far easier to change if it isn't.
